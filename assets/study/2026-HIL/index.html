Thoughts on Human Intelligence and Life

Overfitting to a single point of view can be dangerous

This is a collection of non-academic, personal thoughts on the nature of human intelligence and life. In particular, how do we understand the extraordinary phenomenon where human beings triumph in spite of tragedy and suffering? What role does intelligence and emotion play in this process? Exploring the nature of life and intelligence from multiple perspectives including neuroscience, psychology, sociology, philosophy, and religion.

These are not meant to be rigorous, scientific, or even correct. They are simply my personal efforts to prevent my own understanding from overfitting to a single, technical point of view, and to understand the world in a more holistic way. Like Godel's theorem, I believe that any sufficiently complex system of thought will necessarily be incomplete, and that there will always be truths that are beyond the reach of reason and logic. Therefore, it is important to explore multiple perspectives and to be open to the possibility of truth beyond the knowable and observable.
