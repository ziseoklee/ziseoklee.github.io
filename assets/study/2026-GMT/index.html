Generative Modeling by Transport: Mathematical Foundations

Introduction to Generative Modeling via Measure Transport

Generative modeling fundamentally constitutes the process of sampling from high-dimensional probability distributions. This text formalizes generative processes through the mathematical framework of measure transport, defining data generation as the continuous transformation of a tractable prior distribution into a target empirical distribution via a probability path. This framework provides a unified theoretical basis for diffusion models, normalizing flows, optimal transport models, and Schrödinger bridges.

The paradigm shift from single-step mapping to continuous probability path construction frames data generation as a transport problem governed by the underlying geometry of the data manifold. Consequently, open problems in the field center on determining path optimality, improving computational efficiency in path navigation, and formulating hierarchical compositions of multiple probability paths—referred to herein as collective generative intelligence—to systematically scale system capabilities.

This material aims to integrate the analytical rigor of stochastic processes and statistical physics with applied machine learning. The formal mathematical principles underlying these systems are a necessary prerequisite for expanding their empirical capabilities and theoretical guarantees.

While architectural design and algorithmic implementation remain relevant, this series prioritizes theoretical foundations. A rigorous mathematical formulation provides: (1) a unified taxonomy for seemingly disparate generative models, (2) an explicit characterization of underlying statistical assumptions and topological limitations, and (3) a principled methodology for deriving novel algorithms.

The primary objective of this text is to establish that contemporary state-of-the-art generative models are diverse instantiations of a singular, coherent mathematical framework.
